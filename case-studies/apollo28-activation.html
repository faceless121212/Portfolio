Case study

Tripling activation: 20% → 60%

  • CompanyApollo28
  • RoleProduct Manager
  • TimelineOct 2025 – now
  • LocationWarsaw

TL;DR

Problem
Only 20% of new users activated, meaning they connected to the VPN and browsed in their first session. Most left at the system permission prompt for the VPN.
My role
Product Manager: owned onboarding, experiments, retention strategy and the roadmap for the core browser experience.
Team
One product team: 5–8 engineers and 1–2 designers.
Outcome
Activation 20% → 60% (3x). Average session from under 1 min to 15 min. D7 retention 2x.

Context and why it mattered

Apollo28 is a freemium VPN browser that makes money from ads, with a paid tier that removes them. In an ad-funded product, revenue follows time spent. A user who never connects and browses in their first session never sees value, never comes back and never earns anything. Activation was the constraint on everything else.

Discovery and data

I defined activation as connected + browsed: the user turns on the VPN and visits at least one site in the first session. Then I built the funnel in Amplitude, from install through onboarding and the VPN permission request to the first connection and first site visit.

The biggest drop-off was the system permission prompt. iOS and Android show a technical warning when an app asks to add a VPN configuration, and without any context many users simply backed out.

Hypotheses and prioritization

Hypothesis: if users understand why the app needs the VPN permission before the system prompt appears, more of them will accept it, connect and start browsing.

What I said no to:

  • A full app redesign. The data pointed to one leak, so we fixed that instead of rebuilding everything.
  • New features first. The feature roadmap waited until activation was fixed, because new features don't help users who never get in.
  • An earlier paywall. Users had to reach value before seeing any upsell.

Experiments and execution

  • Pre-permission screen: a short explainer before the system prompt covering what the permission does, why the app needs it and what happens next. This was the main lever for activation.
  • Faster first connection: less time from install to a working connection, so the first session starts with browsing instead of setup.
  • Engagement features that gave users a reason to stay longer in each session.
  • Retention: push reminders for users who didn't return, plus stability fixes for the disconnects and crashes that pushed people away.

Results

MetricBeforeAfter
Activation (connected + browsed)20%60% (3x)
Average session duration< 1 min15 min
D7 retentionbaseline2x

What I learned

Fix the leak first. One drop-off point mattered more than any feature on the roadmap. Fixing the first session tripled activation, and because more people now understood the product, it also doubled how many came back.
