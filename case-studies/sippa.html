Side project

Sippa: an AI companion app, from pitch to live product

  • LinksSide project · Live app · GitHub
  • RoleFounder-PM and builder (solo, AI-assisted)
  • TimelineSep 2026 – now
  • LocationRemote

TL;DR

Problem
AI companion apps are one of the fastest-growing consumer categories, but most feel generic and are vague about safety. Sippa had to be engaging and trustworthy at the same time.
My role
Everything from scope to launch: product decisions (kept in a decision log), specs, design direction, building with Claude, pricing and the growth funnel.
Team
Solo, with AI (Claude for code and in-product chat, fal.ai for portraits and quiz images).
Outcome
Live with 200+ organic users. 60 characters, an AI character creator, chat with memory, Free / Plus / Ultra plans, and a 20-question web-to-app quiz funnel for each audience.

Context and why it mattered

AI companions are one of the fastest-growing consumer app categories, and they monetize through subscriptions and virtual currency. That makes the category a full growth loop (acquisition funnel, activation, monetization and retention), which is the work I do every day, here in a product I own end to end.

Discovery and data

  • Desk research and teardowns of web quiz funnels: Noom, BetterMe, Liven, Lasta and Simple, plus companion apps EVA AI and Replika, and Duolingo for retention.
  • Live walk-throughs of BetterMe, Noom and Simple, stopping before any email or payment step.
  • Feedback sessions on an early version: people said the home page mixed romance, friends and history, and that the planned ad → quiz → match → paywall → chat flow didn't exist yet. That led to three audience pages and a quiz funnel.

Hypotheses and prioritization

Hypothesis: a personal character match from a short quiz converts to sign-up better than a generic landing page.

From a wider review, I picked 13 funnel ideas: two-pole sliders, reactions from your current best match, a "biggest doubt" screen with an honest answer, a "so far" recap, and a typical-chatbot vs. Sippa comparison screen.

What I said no to:

  • Fake countdown timers, scarcity claims, invented statistics or reviews, and an email gate before the result. These are common in the category but count as unfair practices under EU rules.
  • Fake likes. Other AI characters react to posts instead, shown apart from real likes.
  • Social-proof counters before there's real data. They stay hidden until 100 chats and 30 ratings.
  • Voice replies (listed as "coming soon"), Apple sign-in, and any portraits of real people.
  • Interstitial or mid-chat ads.

Experiments and execution

I shipped in six phases, stopping after each one for review: landing page, app shell, chat, AI character creator, monetization, then moderation and polish. Engagement mechanics came next: gifts, bond levels, scenes, a daily check-in, a Moments feed and likes.

  • Performance: replacing Framer Motion with plain CSS for the hero animation took mobile Lighthouse performance from 77 to 95+.
  • Pricing: moved from €7.99 to $13.99 for Plus and added Ultra at $29.99 so the site matched the pitch deck. Plan features now come from a single source so the pricing pages can't drift apart.
  • Funnel hand-off: the opening line of the chat is pre-written for every character × scene × vibe (720 lines), so the quiz result and the real chat start with exactly the same message.
  • Safety by design: 18+ only, no real living people, always labelled as AI, crisis helplines, and moderation that is automated first with human review.

Results

MetricResult
Organic users200+, with no paid traffic
Mobile Lighthouse performance77 → 95+
Shipped scope6 phases plus engagement mechanics

What I learned

Feedback beat my own plan: one round of feedback showed the mixed home page was the problem, and that reshaped acquisition around one audience per page. I also learned that trust can be a growth feature. Refusing dark patterns limits some tactics, but it gives the product a clear reason to be chosen.
