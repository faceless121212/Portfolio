Side project

TrackaAI: a Linear-style tracker with AI teammates

  • LinksSide project · GitHub · launching soon
  • RoleFounder-PM and builder (solo, AI-assisted)
  • TimelineSep 2026 – now
  • LocationRemote

TL;DR

Problem
Small teams lose time writing tickets, splitting work and triaging boards. Linear, Jira, Notion AI and ClickUp AI treat AI as a text box, not as a teammate that does real work.
My role
Wrote the PRD (goals, non-goals, domain model, plans, success metrics), then built it with Claude. It's a full PRD → pricing → product exercise.
Team
Solo, with AI.
Outcome
Working product, launching soon: teams, realtime Kanban boards, five AI features (write, break down, ask, board copilot and AI teammates) and Free / Lite / Pro plans with metered AI runs.

Context and why it mattered

I built TrackaAI to show the whole PM chain in one product: a PRD with measurable goals, packaging and pricing, guardrails for AI, and a shipped app. The bet is that AI can be a real participant on the board. It writes issues, splits them, answers questions and takes assigned work through to review.

Discovery and data

Competitive benchmark:

  • Linear: the bar for speed and keyboard-first UX.
  • Jira: powerful, but heavy for small teams.
  • Notion AI and ClickUp AI: AI is bolted onto docs and tasks as a writing helper.

The gap: nobody treats AI as an assignable teammate with clear permissions and a review step.

Hypotheses and prioritization

Hypothesis: if AI writes the first draft of an issue, more tasks get created with clear acceptance criteria, and teams reach their first board faster.

What I said no to (v1 non-goals): native mobile apps and offline mode; cycles, roadmaps, Gantt charts and time tracking; Git integrations, a public API and webhooks; SSO/SAML and audit logs. I also chose simulated billing first, so pricing could be tested before payment setup.

Experiments and execution

  • Onboarding wizard: create team → first workspace → invite (skippable) → land on a seeded board. The PRD goal is a team working on a board in under 3 minutes.
  • AI with guardrails: every change the copilot wants to make on the board shows up as a card you approve first. AI acts with the permissions of the person using it, and the AI teammate's work always goes to review.
  • Cost-aware models: a cheap model (Haiku) for writing and breakdowns, and a stronger one (Sonnet) for the copilot and agents. Usage is metered per plan in "AI runs".
  • Packaging: Free (1 person, 10 runs a month), Lite ($10: 3 people, 100 runs) and Pro ($25: unlimited, copilot and AI teammates).
  • Built to swap backends: a mock data layer shipped first, so the UI could be built before the database. Supabase was then swapped in without UI changes.

What I learned

Writing non-goals is as important as writing goals: the v1 list kept a solo build shippable. And AI features need product design, not just prompts. Approval cards, permissions and usage metering are what make AI safe to give to a team.
